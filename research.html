Research

As a researcher, I’m drawn to understanding the relationship between the mind and the human capacity for language, specifically when it comes to implicit knowledge of human speech sounds. Here are some of the questions that keep me up at night:

  • What is the content of stored representations of speech sounds?
  • What content do phonological computations operate over?
  • What kinds of phonological operations are possible?
  • How does a learner achieve mastery of the speech sounds in their environment?
  • How do we account for the influence of distributional, perceptual, and articulatory factors on the storage, comprehension, and production of speech sounds?

My approach is fundamentally informed by psycholinguistic approaches (“What can we learn about language from behavior?”) and Marr's (1982) levels of analysis within a generativist Chomskian framework.

At the moment, I'm interested in how information theoretic models can provide insights into the nature of human phonological representations and computations.

Specific research topics that I've worked on include:

  • Accounting for similarity effects in consonant harmony
  • Verbal morphophonology in Amharic
  • Acquisition of subject agreement morphology in Tz’utujil Mayan
  • Infant speech perception of stop contrasts
  • The influence of English-Spanish code-switching on children’s language development
